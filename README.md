# Network-walks-B083-week2-cyber-security-and-ethical-hacking-
Week 2 of my Cybersecurity & Ethical Hacking internship at Networkwalks is officially in the books, and it was such an exciting one! 

This week was all about mastering the art of reconnaissance, footprinting and scanning—truly the foundation of every penetration test. Under the fantastic guidance of sir Waqas Karim (CCIE) , I dove deep into how security professionals and attackers alike gather vital information before ever touching a target.
Here is a look at what I worked on:

External Footprinting (Kali Linux):

🔹WHOIS for uncovering domain registration and hosting details.
🔹WhatWeb to identify the CMS and specific plugin versions.
🔹Nslookup for resolving the target domain to its public IP.
🔹Curl -I to inspect headers and spot exposed endpoints like the WordPress REST API.
🔹Wafw00f to detect the Web Application Firewall protecting the site.
🔹DNSRecon to thoroughly map out DNS records (SOA, NS, MX, TXT, SRV).

Local Network Scanning (Zenmap / Nmap):

✅ Scanned my local subnet for live hosts (adapting to a non-standard /28 range via my mobile hotspot!).
✅ Discovered active devices, capturing their IP and MAC addresses.
✅ Identified the router gateway and observed modern security features like MAC randomisation.
✅ Generated a clean, visual network topology.

- Biggest Takeaway:
Reconnaissance alone can reveal a target's CMS, plugin versions, exposed API endpoints, mail infrastructure, and defensive setup—all completely passively, without a single exploit. It showed me firsthand why reconnaissance forms the foundation of every single security assessment 

A special thanks  to sir Waqas Karim (CCIE)  and the entire NETWORKWALKS team for providing such structured, hands-on labs. This is the kind of practical learning that genuinely sticks!
